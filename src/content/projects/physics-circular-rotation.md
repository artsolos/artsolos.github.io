## Goal and my contribution

These experiments compare timing-based observations with models of circular and rotational motion. My partner and I followed the procedures and collected the measurements together. Report analysis is presented as shared evidence; the apparatus was used for course experiments, not designed by me.

## Force and circular motion

The circular-motion experiment used a Sargent-Welch apparatus with a rotating bob, a rubber-band restoring force, and a measured path radius of **0.187 m**. Stopwatch readings over several revolutions supplied a period, from which speed was calculated. Force was calculated from the applied calibration masses.

The original force-versus-squared-speed graph has a linear fitted trend, consistent with the intended model `F = mv²/r`. That is a relationship between calculated quantities based on measured masses, radius, and times. The graph's fit is **F ≈ 2.468v² − 0.1915** in the report's SI context; the slope represents `m/r`, not an acceleration. Its nonzero intercept also limits the source's claim that the fitted line passes through the origin. No independent gravity or accuracy result is claimed.

## Maxwell’s wheel

The wheel experiment first measured mass and disk/axle dimensions with a scale and caliper to build a geometric inertia model. Timing trials then followed the wheel as its supporting strings unwound. The report documents **2, 4, 6, and 8 turns**, with **five descent times per condition**, followed by a comparison with attached brass knobs.

The two original distance-versus-squared-time plots show a smaller fitted slope with the knobs. This is the supported trend: a changed mass distribution accompanies a slower timed descent in the reported comparison. Calculated acceleration and inferred inertia are distinct from the directly measured times and dimensions.

## Measurement limits

Stopwatch reaction time, manual spinning, radius measurement, string winding, and wheel wobble limit the observations. The wheel tables use both a measured drop height and a calculated unwound length, giving different acceleration columns. Its slope wording, inertia units, and summarized values are also inconsistent. The portfolio preserves those source distinctions without selecting an invented corrected inertia value or endorsing the claimed agreement with theory.

The new schematic explains the procedure only. The plots are original report evidence; no new laboratory run, regression fit, or uncertainty validation was performed during this update.
