## Goal and my contribution

These experiments use tracked position to examine work, energy, and momentum. I followed the procedures and collected data together with the lab partners: a two-person air-track experiment and a three-person collision experiment. Figures and analysis are shared report evidence, without assigning me sole ownership of the calculations.

## Air-track work and energy

The air-track study compared a glider on an incline with a horizontal glider pulled by a hanging mass over a pulley. A PASCO motion sensor and Capstone recorded position over time. Excel calculations formed velocity from successive samples, squared it, and paired it with average position for fitted plots.

The intended work-energy model predicts a linear relationship between squared speed and displacement under the stated ideal conditions. The report documents a **0.374 kg glider** and **0.030 kg hanging mass** for the pulley run. These are component measurements; fitted slopes and inferred gravity values are calculated results.

The source contains substantial conflicts: its incline geometry and arithmetic do not reproduce the stated gravity estimate, and its pulley plot, prose slopes, and substituted calculation disagree. The claimed **9.42 m/s²** and **3.98% error** are therefore not presented as verified results. Friction and sensor alignment are discussed as possible experimental limitations, but they do not resolve contradictory arithmetic.

## Hover-puck collisions

The collision study used video tracking on a glass surface, with wall rebounds, two-puck interactions, and a Velcro collision in which the pucks stuck together. Tracker and Excel supported the analysis. Position changes supplied velocity estimates; measured masses and derived velocities supplied momentum components and kinetic energy.

The report compares individual and total component series before and after collision, then examines center-of-mass motion. Its inelastic-collision energy plot shows a declining central region, while also containing substantial scatter and a late outlier. Momentum totals are not uniformly flat. These observations are more limited than the report's broad conclusions, so no numerical energy-loss percentage or verified momentum-conservation tolerance is claimed.

## Evidence limits

Release alignment, tracking consistency, numerical differentiation, and selecting a valid pre/post-collision interval matter to both studies. The reports discuss these issues, but raw tracking files and spreadsheet workbooks were not included. The public copies preserve the original technical pages, including their conflicting conclusions. This update reviewed the evidence; it did not reconstruct missing data, refit trajectories, or repeat an experiment.
