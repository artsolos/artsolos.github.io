## Business question and my role

Could a cereal positioned around retaining crunch in milk address a family breakfast frustration? EverCrunch explores that question as a **hypothetical course concept**, combining a one-page product concept with an eleven-page final marketing plan dated April 16, 2026.

I completed both submissions individually: developing the idea, selecting the audience, using secondary sources, proposing the marketing mix, and estimating a launch scenario. The completed deliverable is a written academic plan. There was no product launch or testing.

## Audience, research, and positioning

The plan targets **parents and guardians of children ages 5–12**, particularly families with busy mornings or slow eaters. It distinguishes the parent who buys cereal from the child who consumes it and influences the purchase. The positioning centers on texture over time: a proposed moisture-resistant coating would slow milk absorption. Convenience and less uneaten cereal are intended benefits, not measured improvements.

Research draws on published breakfast/category commentary, marketing concepts, and course material. The bibliography provides context for convenience, purchasing behavior, and category choices; it does not establish demand for EverCrunch. No interviews, survey data, willingness-to-pay study, or competitive validation were supplied. Later portfolio checks covered two cited web pages only, with reference-date/version limits noted in the source links.

## Recommended marketing mix

- **Product:** a ready-to-eat cereal with the proposed coating, kid-friendly flavors and shapes, bright packaging, an aquatic-animal mascot idea, and a satisfaction guarantee.
- **Price:** value-based positioning at a proposed $5–6 retail price, using about $5.50 as the planning anchor. Customer acceptance was not tested.
- **Place:** grocery and big-box retail, with online availability also proposed. The report outlines a manufacturer–distributor–retailer channel without claiming retailer agreements.
- **Promotion:** parent-focused social media and influencers, child-oriented video/TV creative, coupons, and store displays. These are campaign recommendations with no measured reach, conversion, or sales results.

The strategy connects the audience distinction to both the message and channel choices: parents receive a convenience proposition, while child-facing creative emphasizes enjoyment. The supplied files contain text rather than packaging artwork or a presentation deck.

## Financial scenario and supported outcome

The report assumes 500 stores selling 30 boxes monthly for twelve months, or **180,000 boxes**, at approximately **$3.02 wholesale revenue per box**.
