## Business question and my role

How do Columbia Sportswear's profitability, working capital, and financing position inform a lender's or investor's view, especially alongside Under Armour? Parts 1 and 2 address that question as one connected ACCT1209 project.

I completed both parts as individual coursework: company research, financial calculations, comparative interpretation, stakeholder conclusions, and the AI-output critique. The later portfolio source checks and visualization are identified separately from my original submissions.

## From statements to analysis

Part 1 reviews the business, revenue and earnings trends, gross margin, major asset categories, inventory turnover, financing mix, and selected accounting policies and footnotes. Part 2 uses profitability, liquidity, turnover, and capital-structure ratios to compare the companies and develop stakeholder conclusions.

The methods include year-over-year percentage changes, profit divided by sales, current assets divided by current liabilities, and cost of sales divided by average inventory. Common-size balance-sheet percentages connect the amounts to the financing structure. Monetary units and reporting dates matter: some source tables use USD thousands while the narrative uses millions.

## Supported findings

The reports show Columbia's sales remaining near $3.4 billion in 2022–2025 while net income declines. For 2025, Part 1 gives **$3,397.4 million sales** and **$177.2 million net income**. Part 2 compares that income with **$282.9 million operating cash flow**. These selected amounts agree, at the reports' rounding, with Columbia's [2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1050797/000105079726000028/colm-20251231.htm).

## Academic conclusions and evidence limits

Part 2 proposes a moderate, collateral-backed short-term loan and a stock **hold** conclusion within the class exercise. Those are historical report conclusions, not an actual lending decision or a current investment recommendation. The report balances Columbia's financial position against declining earnings.

Several qualifications remain visible. The report's 0.71 “debt-to-equity” value numerically uses total liabilities divided by equity, which differs from interest-bearing debt alone. Average-versus-year-end denominators and quick-asset definitions need clearer specification. Part 1's retained-earnings discussion does not capture the year-end decrease in the filing; the original report is preserved rather than silently rewritten.

The AI reflection identifies vague comparisons and missing years/numbers, then describes a revised prompt. Full AI-response transcripts are absent, so the claimed improvement in accuracy is not independently established. This portfolio update checked selected figures and definitions; it is not a complete audit of the submissions.
