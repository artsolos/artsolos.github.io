## Goal and my contribution

These partner labs connect spatial voltage measurements with electric-field models, then examine voltage and current in DC circuits. My partner and I followed the procedures and collected data together. The report calculations are shared evidence; no individual ownership of the analysis is inferred.

## Mapping electric potential

A **10 V supply**, conducting paper, and a digital multimeter were used with parallel and concentric electrodes. The team located equipotential positions and used the potential change over distance to estimate field components. Holding the paper flat and maintaining probe contact mattered: finite pencil-line width, contact variation, and edge effects limited how precisely a position could be assigned.

The parallel-electrode graph distinguishes measured potential from the report's calculated comparison series. For concentric electrodes, the report uses a logarithmic potential model based on electrode radii. These models are predictions for the stated geometry, not additional measured data.

The report's abstract, field calculations, and percentage-error descriptions do not consistently agree. The portfolio therefore emphasizes the mapping procedure and plotted observations rather than repeating a numerical accuracy claim.

## Battery and resistor measurements

The DC lab checked multimeter mode, input jack, range, and polarity before measuring batteries and resistor networks. The source records **1.512 V for one battery**, **3.013 V for two in series**, and **1.499 V for two in parallel**. These are original measured voltages; the ideal series/parallel rules are the theoretical comparison.

A nominal 100 Ω resistor measured **98.65 Ω** directly. Voltage and current readings supplied a separate `V/I` estimate. Series and parallel networks using measured **470.2 Ω and 1054.3 Ω** components added checks of voltage division and branch currents. Resistances calculated from `V/I`, component-sum predictions, and direct ohmmeter readings are different kinds of evidence and are not treated as interchangeable measurements.

## Troubleshooting and evidence limits

The report documents voltage across the ammeter, making meter burden part of the setup rather than assuming an ideal instrument. Connection losses, probe contact, and instrument ranges are practical limits on the comparison.

The battery internal-resistance value does not follow from the listed voltage and current values without further explanation. Equivalent-resistance summaries also differ between sections. Those disputed results are not promoted as performance claims. This update reviewed the reports and selected arithmetic; it did not repeat the experiments or establish new uncertainty bounds. Privacy-reviewed downloads retain the original technical pages so these limits remain visible.
